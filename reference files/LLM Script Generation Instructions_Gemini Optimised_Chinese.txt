STRUCTURE TO FOLLOW

1. Introduction / Hook (≈30-45 seconds)
   • Begin with a concrete moment, detail, or contrast that catches attention immediately. No broad metaphors, no clichés.
   • Present the central problem or question of the episode in plain, compelling terms.
   • Give 3-4 learning outcomes in conversational language: "By the end, you'll see how…"
   • End with one curiosity-provoking question.

2. Overview / Roadmap (≈30 seconds)
   • Give a short, natural-sounding outline of the episode's flow.
   • Use bridging phrases ("First we look at…, then…, finally…"). Avoid formal enumeration tone.

3. Chronology (≈1-2 minutes)
   • Present key events as a brief narrative sequence rather than a list.
   • Group dates where practical. Avoid unnecessary date repetition.
   • Emphasise cause and effect.

4. Main Parts (several, as required)
   • Start each part with a grounded detail, anecdote, or condition that sets the scene.
   • Recommended Revision: Use active voice, strong verbs, and varied sentence lengths (up to 35 words). 
   • Maintain a fluid rhythm, balancing short, punchy statements with longer, more detailed sentences..
   • Maintain tight information economy; remove generic or broad-stroke statements.
   • Include 1-2 concise reflective questions.
   • Highlight contrasts where helpful.
   • End each part with a clear, one-sentence takeaway.

5. Synthesis / Reflection (≈1-2 minutes)
   • Draw together patterns, tensions, or turning points.
   • Show how the themes relate without repeating earlier sentences.
   • Include 1-2 thoughtful questions that prompt deeper consideration.

6. Recap & Memory Anchors (≈45 seconds)
   • Restate 4-6 essential points in crisp, fresh phrasing.
   • Use a single metaphor, image, or conceptual anchor that is not a cliché.

7. Key Names, Places, Events, Things (≈45 seconds)
   • Present up to eight items.
   • Number them from one to eight.
   • Give each a brief, vivid identifying phrase.

8. Closing (≈20-30 seconds)
   • Offer a reflective closing line that feels complete.
   • No summaries, no clichés, no vague uplift.
